# THE IRON MIND — BRAND MASTER
Version: 1.0
Purpose: Source file for AVENAN Voice Intelligence Engine Brand DNA analysis.

## 1. BRAND IDENTITY
Channel Name: THE IRON MIND

Core Idea:
A faceless motivation channel about discipline, identity, resilience, self-command, and mental strength.

Core Promise:
Every video should confront the viewer with an uncomfortable truth, create self-recognition, and push them toward disciplined action.

Brand Essence:
Pressure reveals weakness. Discipline rebuilds identity.

Brand Personality:
- Direct
- Intense
- Controlled
- Intelligent
- Reflective
- Grounded
- Non-preachy
- Non-hysterical
- Never fake-aggressive

Emotional Signature:
“Đánh đau nhưng thức tỉnh.”
The viewer should feel seen, challenged, slightly uncomfortable, then mentally clearer and more determined.

## 2. TARGET AUDIENCE
Primary audience:
- English-speaking viewers
- Mostly 18–40
- People struggling with procrastination, inconsistency, low discipline, identity conflict, burnout, self-doubt, distraction, fear of failure, and lack of direction
- Viewers who consume motivation, self-improvement, productivity, discipline, mindset, and personal development content

Psychological state:
- Knows what should be done but repeatedly fails to act
- Feels behind in life
- Feels mentally overloaded
- Wants control, clarity, confidence, and consistency
- Responds better to truth and structure than empty positivity

## 3. POSITIONING
THE IRON MIND is not generic motivational hype.

It is:
- Narrative-driven
- Voice-first
- Psychological
- Identity-centered
- Discipline-centered
- Visually minimal
- Symbolic rather than literal
- Built around insight, tension, reveal, and internal confrontation

Avoid:
- Generic “you can do anything” content
- Loud alpha-male clichés
- Fake luxury
- Hustle-porn
- Empty quotes
- Polarizing ideology
- Political messaging
- Religious persuasion
- Shaming vulnerable viewers

## 5. NARRATIVE SYSTEM
Voice Is the Master Timeline.
Audio determines timing.
Text clarifies semantics.
Visuals support meaning.
Visuals must never dictate narration timing.

Preferred Narrative Functions:
HOOK
SETUP
TENSION
QUESTION
EVIDENCE
REVEAL
REFLECTION
TRANSITION
CTA

Typical Emotional Arc:
Recognition → discomfort → tension → insight → reveal → clarity → action.

Hook Rules:
- Create self-recognition fast
- Expose a contradiction
- Name a hidden problem
- Challenge a comforting belief
- Avoid generic motivational openings

Reveal Rules:
- Change interpretation
- Reframe the problem
- Make the viewer mentally pause
- Feel earned, not dramatic for its own sake

CTA Rules:
- Behavioral and identity-driven
- One concrete next action
- One decision
- One identity statement
- One rule to apply immediately

## 6. SCRIPT STYLE
Primary language: English (US)

Writing Style:
- Short to medium sentences
- Strong rhythm
- Deliberate pauses
- Controlled repetition
- Concrete words
- Low fluff
- Minimal jargon
- Clear transitions
- Strong contrast structures

Tone:
- Calm pressure
- Serious
- Precise
- Reflective
- Mature
- Never melodramatic

Avoid:
- Excessive metaphors
- Constant shouting language
- Overuse of “warrior”, “beast”, “king”, “alpha”
- Corporate language
- Therapy clichés
- Infantilizing language

## 7. VOICE GUIDE
Voice Personality:
- Male voice preferred
- Young to middle-age adult
- Controlled confidence
- Slightly dark tone
- Clear articulation
- Not theatrical
- Not radio-announcer style
- Not overly emotional

Pace:
- Moderate
- Slow down on reveals
- Faster on setup/tension only when appropriate
- Use silence intentionally

Pause Strategy:
- <180 ms: no visual change
- 180–320 ms: possible micro-motion
- 320–700 ms: phrase-level visual shift
- 700–1000 ms: strong pause, possible shot change
- >1000 ms: dramatic silence hold or major visual emphasis

## 8. VISUAL PHILOSOPHY
Visuals are not the main product.
Narration and script are the main product.

Visuals should:
- Support meaning
- Maintain attention
- Create mood
- Reinforce symbolic ideas
- Stay inexpensive and repeatable
- Avoid unnecessary complexity

A single visual may span multiple phrases.
Prefer motion inside a composition before cutting to a new scene.

## 9. VISUAL DNA — HARD LOCK
Core Style:
Modern digital etched-ink illustration on a pure or near-black canvas.

Background:
- Pure black or near-black
- Black should dominate 80–90% of the frame
- Large negative space is desirable
- No visible paper texture

Linework:
- Thin irregular etched ink / graphite-like scratch lines
- Cool grey and muted-white contours
- Controlled cross-hatching
- Fine, modern, digital-feeling linework
- No broad charcoal strokes

Subject Rendering:
- Mostly black-filled silhouette or dark mass
- Believable contemporary anatomy
- Details revealed through edge light and thin contour scratches
- Avoid fully illuminated figures

Color Ratio:
- 85–90% black / near-black
- 7–10% cool grey / muted white
- Maximum 3–5% amber/orange accent

Accent Color:
Warm amber/orange only for:
- Rim light
- Tiny practical lights
- Distant city windows
- Screen glow
- Emotional emphasis

Never use orange as a full-scene grade.

Lighting:
- High-contrast
- Low-key
- Sparse rim lighting
- Deep blacks remain deep black
- Light carves the subject out of darkness

Composition:
- Cinematic 16:9
- Strong silhouette
- Simple focal hierarchy
- Large negative space
- Minimal clutter
- Modern framing
- Visual restraint

Era:
Contemporary unless narration explicitly requires otherwise.

Preferred props/environments:
- Laptop
- Desk
- Phone
- Apartment
- Office
- City window
- Transit
- Bedroom
- Street at night
- Gym
- Modern interiors

## 10. VISUAL FORBIDDEN LIST
Never generate unless narration explicitly requires:
- Beige background
- Cream background
- White paper
- Parchment
- Aged paper
- Sketchbook page
- Sepia
- Brown wash
- Vintage illustration
- Traditional charcoal-on-paper look
- Watercolor
- Oil painting
- Storybook illustration
- Antique interiors
- Old books as decorative default
- Candles as primary light source
- Quills
- Historical furniture
- Decorative paper borders
- Fake CGI gloss
- Plastic-looking surfaces
- Overly detailed cinematic realism

## 11. KEYFRAME PROMPT RULES
Every Keyframe Prompt must:
1. Follow narration meaning.
2. Preserve Brand DNA.
3. Preserve project Visual DNA.
4. Use one clear focal idea.
5. Avoid visual clutter.
6. Keep modern environment when narration is modern.
7. Use black-dominant composition.
8. Use restrained amber accents.
9. Avoid readable generated text/logos.

Mandatory Prompt Prefix:
“Modern digital etched-ink illustration on a pure near-black canvas, black-filled forms, thin muted-white and cool-grey scratch contours, extremely restrained amber rim-light accents, large negative space, contemporary cinematic 16:9 composition.”

Mandatory Negative Lock:
“NO beige or white paper, NO parchment, NO sepia, NO vintage sketchbook, NO broad charcoal-on-paper rendering, NO watercolor, NO oil painting, NO antique props, NO candlelit historical mood unless narration explicitly requires it.”

## 12. VIDEO MOTION PROMPT RULES
Motion must preserve the keyframe.
Do not redesign the composition.

Preferred motion:
- Slow push-in
- Small posture shifts
- Breathing
- Slight head movement
- Subtle hand movement
- Screen glow
- Distant city light flicker
- Tiny floating particles
- Very light parallax
- Slow shadow movement

Avoid:
- Large camera moves
- Fast cuts
- Morphing
- Rebuilding scene geometry
- New objects entering unexpectedly
- Character redesign
- Style drift

## 13. VISUAL ACTION POLICY
Available actions:
HOLD
MICRO_MOTION
COMPOSITION_CHANGE
SHOT_CHANGE
SCENE_CHANGE
SILENCE_HOLD

Priority:
HOLD > MICRO_MOTION > COMPOSITION_CHANGE > SHOT_CHANGE > SCENE_CHANGE

Only cut when meaning genuinely changes.

## 14. CONTINUITY RULES
Maintain continuity across adjacent beats:
- Same character identity
- Same clothing unless story changes
- Same environment when narration continues same idea
- Same palette
- Same linework
- Same lighting logic
- Same camera language
- Same emotional tone

Do not create a new visual world for every sentence.

## 15. PRODUCTION ECONOMY
Primary objective:
Maximum narrative impact with minimum visual production cost.

Prefer:
- Reusable visual grammar
- Simple compositions
- Minimal moving parts
- Subtle animation
- Repeatable scene archetypes

Avoid:
- Complex crowd scenes
- Highly detailed environments
- Expensive photorealism
- Long action sequences
- Constant visual novelty

## 16. BRAND DNA PRIORITY
When generating any visual prompt, use priority:
1. BRAND DNA
2. PROJECT STYLE DNA
3. CHANNEL PROFILE
4. NARRATIVE BEAT
5. MODEL DEFAULTS

If lower-level rules conflict with Brand DNA, Brand DNA wins.

## 17. QUALITY CONTROL
A valid THE IRON MIND visual should pass all checks:
- Does it look modern?
- Is the frame mostly black?
- Are line details thin and restrained?
- Is amber used sparingly?
- Is there strong negative space?
- Is the composition simple?
- Does it support the narration?
- Does it avoid paper/sepia/vintage drift?
- Does it preserve continuity?
- Could it be animated cheaply?

If any answer is “No”, regenerate or revise the prompt.
